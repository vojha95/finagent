## Reading the scoped DSCR (notes for Vineet)

- **A negative ratio means modelled CFO plus interest is below capex** (negative cash flow available for debt service), not a sign error. It is shown as computed, never clamped, and labelled "capex exceeds modelled operating cash flow" (#169, reviewer decision pending Vineet). It is "n.m." only when the debt service is not positive. After BC27 only AMZN is negative, in every scenario and year: for example, Base FY2026 has CFO of $117.7bn against capex of $147.3bn. NKE Severe and T Severe FY+1 were negative before BC27 and are now 0.16x and 0.26x.
- **Interest is added back (#168, BC27, confirmed by Vineet).** The model's CFO starts from net income, which is after funded interest. The numerator is now the standard cash flow available for debt service: CFO + funded and finance-lease interest − capex. The denominator is unchanged. The second calculation (closing cash − opening cash − CFF + the model's interest line) and the workbook rows (`f:ds_cfads`, `f:scoped_dscr`, `chk:dscr`) follow the same formula. The BC27 before/after (credit-0001 → credit-0002): every ratio rose, by 0.14x (ABT Base FY+1) to 1.00x (NKE FY+2 and FY+3). credit-0003 (after #152 and D1, BC28) is identical to credit-0002.
- **Base refinances maturities**, yet scheduled principal is counted gross (D6, #163). So a Base DSCR below 1x does not mean the model shows a cash shortfall; the liquidity rows show that.
- **Year-to-year pattern.** In Moderate and Severe, FY+1 is usually the weakest year: the scenario hits CFO hardest in FY+1 (AMZN Severe CFO is $41.9bn in FY2026 vs $84.3bn in FY2027), and funded interest is higher. The scheduled maturities also move the ratio. For example, MSFT has $9.25bn of principal in FY2027 and none in FY2028 (2.57x to 6.73x in Base), and NKE has $2.0bn in FY2027 and none after. CVX Base is the exception: it is strongest in FY+1, with less principal due that year.
