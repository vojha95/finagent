## D1 (#154): headroom basis options and their effect (decision pending Vineet)

The basis is one switch, `configs/forecast.yaml` `switches.headroom_basis`; the default stays `cash_plus_haircut_securities` (4c) until Vineet decides, so no existing output changes. Both measures are always shown (the tables above, `liquidity.csv`, the workbook's liquidity block).

- **cash_only** (8.12: cash - floor). Follows 8.12 and Appendix B F5: the conditional securities reserve is shown separately and never counted as cash. Headroom is computable for every company, including one whose short-term investments are not accepted.
- **cash_plus_haircut_securities** (4c: cash + securities x (1 - haircut) - floor). The 4c decision (liquidity = cash + short-term investments, #84). Needs accepted short-term investments, otherwise the headroom is not computed (HEADROOM_NOT_COMPUTED) and the conditional debt goal's condition is unknown.

The conditional debt goal ("if there is a shortfall, check debt service") runs when the liquidity goal's `liquidity_shortfall` in the target year (default FY+1, the question may name +2 or +3) is above 0; with no shortfall it is SKIPPED; with no verified shortfall value its condition is unknown (GAP CONDITION_UNKNOWN). ## Reading the scoped DSCR (notes for Vineet)

- **A negative ratio means modelled CFO is below capex** (negative free cash flow before debt service), not a sign error. AMZN is negative in every scenario and year; for example, Base FY2026 has CFO of $117.7bn against capex of $147.3bn. NKE Severe and T Severe are negative in FY+1. The ratio is shown as computed; it is "n.m." only when the debt service is not positive (#169).
- **Interest sits on both sides (#168).** The model's CFO starts from net income, which is after funded interest. So interest is deducted in the numerator and counted again in the denominator. Kept as written in 8.12 (CFO − capex). A cash-flow-available-for-debt-service convention would add interest paid back to the numerator. That is a decision for Vineet, not changed here.
- **Base refinances maturities**, yet scheduled principal is counted gross (D6, #163). So a Base DSCR below 1x does not mean the model shows a cash shortfall; the liquidity rows show that.
- **Year-to-year pattern.** In Moderate and Severe, FY+1 is usually the weakest year: the scenario hits CFO hardest in FY+1 (AMZN Severe CFO is $41.9bn in FY2026 vs $84.3bn in FY2027), and funded interest is higher. The scheduled maturities also move the ratio. For example, MSFT has $9.25bn of principal in FY2027 and none in FY2028 (2.38x to 6.39x in Base), and NKE has $2.0bn in FY2027 and none after. CVX Base is the exception: it is strongest in FY+1, with less principal due that year.
